\subsubsection{Efficiency}\label{sec:cost-dataloc}
\fref{fig:cost_analysis-dataloc} compares average execution time and token consumption on \dataset, where the y-axis denotes execution time and bubble size represents token usage in thousands. \tooldataloc with Claude-3.5 achieves a favorable trade-off between efficiency and effectiveness, completing each task in 39.3s on average with 16.2k tokens. Although slightly slower than SweRank (Small) at 33.5s, \tooldataloc achieves significantly better performance on KA-LCL with low token consumption.

\paragraph{Temporal efficiency.}
Among the evaluated pipeline-based and agentic localization methods, \tooldataloc with Claude-3.5 is the fastest, requiring about half the runtime of the second-fastest baseline, LocAgent with GPT-4o (71.9s). Longer runtimes do not yield better KA-LCL performance, suggesting that, without keyword shortcuts, baselines fail to conduct meaningful repository-level inference despite producing intermediate reasoning traces. \tooldataloc with Qwen3-Max is slower mainly because its frequent Datalog syntax violations trigger more correction, diagnostics, and mutation-based feedback. Although these steps increase runtime overhead, the improved validity and localization precision demonstrate the effectiveness of the mechanism.

\paragraph{Token economy.}
Token consumption varies substantially across methods and foundation models. As shown in \fref{fig:cost_analysis-dataloc}, LocAgent consumes 343.9k tokens with GPT-4o and 393.0k with Claude-3.5, over an order of magnitude more than the 16.2k tokens used by \tooldataloc with Claude-3.5. Agentless, however, uses 13.3k and 15.2k tokens with these models, respectively, slightly fewer than \tooldataloc with Claude-3.5. With Qwen3-Max, \tooldataloc consumes 36.4k tokens per task, also exceeding the token consumption of the two Agentless configurations. Thus, the observed token savings are specific to the model and baseline being compared. Multi-round exploration can be costly; for example, in the evaluation, more than ten queries caused LocAgent to enter repeated loops without producing results.

The efficiency of \tooldataloc stems from its hybrid architecture. In the design, the LLM-based agent is strategically confined to high-level tasks: query analysis, Datalog program synthesis, and final candidate verification. By delegating deep inference to a specialized engine, \tooldataloc greatly reduces the overhead of redundant multi-round exploration. In addition, errors carry a small cost, requiring only the regeneration of a Datalog program. Beyond this low overhead, the parser-gated validation and intermediate-rule feedback actively assist the LLM in synthesizing high-quality programs.
